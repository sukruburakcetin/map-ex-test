I understand it as a request for a structured review of railway crossings, with street imagery as evidence.

Their intended workflow seems to be:
1. Select a crossing on the map and see its identity, name, and existing rating.
2. Review images approaching it from both sides, ideally as a slideshow.
3. Let students or peers discuss visibility, signage, barriers, and other hazards.
4. Record a risk opinion—High risk, Low risk, or No risk—alongside accident statistics.
They also report a separate reliability problem: their Mapillary links stopped working or opened black images.

The connection to our notes is strong: a note could represent an assessment of a specific crossing, linked to relevant images and containing observations, a rating, reviewer, and review date. Multiple assessments could belong to the same crossing.

Two distinctions matter:
- Workflow status and risk rating are separate. A review can be “Completed” while the crossing is rated “High risk.”
- A peer’s visual assessment and accident statistics are different evidence sources. Neither should silently stand in for the other.
I would interpret “No risk” cautiously: street imagery can support “No obvious hazards observed,” but cannot establish that a crossing has no risk.

So the underlying feature is broader than categorizing notes: it is location-based assessment with guided imagery review and recorded evidence.

We can support them by adding a shared community workspace alongside Local and Enterprise notes. Users would need no Esri Enterprise account.

The key distinction is that local notes live in one browser. Worldwide collaboration requires a shared service to store records, identify contributors, and control who can change them. That service can be independent of Esri.

I’d offer three choices:
Notes source	Intended use
Local	Personal work, offline drafts, importing/exporting files
Community workspace	Classes, research groups, and public contribution projects
Enterprise	Organizations using their own ArcGIS services


For the railway-crossing example, someone could create a workspace, share an invitation link, and let students contribute through the distributed demo. Each crossing would have one location record, with multiple assessments beneath it. One person’s opinion would not overwrite everyone else’s.

A practical community service would provide:
- Public viewing, with sign-in required to submit assessments.
- Public or invitation-only workspaces, including private classroom projects.
- Contributor identity, timestamps, edit history, and reporting/moderation.
- Local drafts that users explicitly submit when ready.
- Export so contributors can take their work elsewhere.
Mapillary image IDs and links could accompany observations; we should check Mapillary’s current terms before storing or redistributing imagery itself.

For implementation, a small hosted database and authentication service—such as Supabase—would be a reasonable starting point. The widget could use a common notes interface with separate Local, Community, and Enterprise adapters, keeping service-specific code out of the panel.

My recommendation is to start with invitation-only community workspaces, hosted for the demo and optionally self-hosted by others. That enables real collaboration with manageable ownership and moderation. A completely open worldwide contribution map can follow once those foundations work.

This would make the demo a usable collaboration tool, but it also introduces an ongoing responsibility: someone must operate the shared service and manage its costs and contributions.
